## 中文摘要 (Executive Summary)

**结论：棋力差不是"评估项没调好"或"剪枝启发式不够"，而是三个结构性问题叠加。**

1. **吞吐量天花板**：纯 Python 引擎实测约 1–2 万节点/秒（每节点约 414 次 Python 函数调用）。
   在与 Pikafish 的等时对局中，AlphaZeta 平均只能完成 1.4–2.1 层（Pikafish 报告 45–59 层）。
   V0.9.9 当时已指出"下一步收益是架构性的，不是再微调"，但 V0.10 后续又回到了微优化。
2. **浅层搜索不可靠**：对 Pikafish 的 500 ms 对局中，**27% 的着法是"深度 0 回退"**（连 1 层搜索
   都没算完，直接返回走法生成器里第一个合法着法，近似乱走）；从 10 ply 的开局局面算起，平均只有 30–35 ply（约 15–17 回合）就告负。
   原因：静态搜索无剪枝/排序、1 层搜索未完成时没有安全兜底。
3. **度量体系分辨不出正在追逐的改进**：每个实验 16–96 局，95% 区间 ±60–150 Elo，没有 SPRT。
   所以 null-move、LMR、aspiration、quiet-check、SEE 等实验全部"中性"，一直 opt-in。
   而 +20 Elo 的改动需要约 930 局才能分辨——Python 引擎单核要跑 14 小时。

另有已**实测验证**的实战缺陷：UCCI 对局时引擎看不到真实的重复局面历史（真实历史长度 9，搜索线程
看到的是 1）；"长将/长捉"规则未实现；`go time` 被默认深度 5 封顶；Web / CLI / UCCI / 评测脚本
实际运行的不是同一套配置。

**方案（四个阶段，详见 §7）**

| 阶段 | 主题 | 一句话 |
|---|---|---|
| V0.11 | 度量与实战缺陷 | 统一对局工具 + SPRT + Pikafish 节点阶梯 + 战术/失误测试集；修复"乱走/失忆"；冻结规则语义 |
| V0.12 | 原生核心 | 热路径迁到原生代码（优先 C++17），Python 实现保留为"可执行规格"做差分测试；吞吐量 ≥ 50×  |

MCTS / 策略网络路线**冻结为研究分支**（现有结果为负，且缺少训练规模与基础设施）。每个阶段都给出

## 1. Scope and non-goals

In scope: diagnosing why the engine is weak, ranking the causes by evidence, defining a target
architecture, and specifying phased work with measurable acceptance criteria.

Non-goals: no code is changed here; previous experiment results are not re-litigated (they are
used as evidence); no UI redesign; no Elo claim is made beyond what was measured. Anything
labelled *hypothesis* or *estimate* is a planning number to be replaced by a measured one at the
milestone where it is tested.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 What the recorded Pikafish matches show

Every AlphaZeta move in the four recorded Pikafish matches carries `search_stats`
(completed depth, elapsed ms). Re-aggregated for this review:

| Run (`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_…`) | AZ moves | depth 0 | depth ≤ 1 | mean depth | mean game length |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 500 ms, 10 pairs (`500ms_10openings`) | 298 | **26.5%** | 67.1% | 1.36 (1.15¹) | 30 plies |
| 500 ms, post-qsearch | 329 | 13.7% | 46.5% | 1.77 (1.58¹) | 33 plies |
| 500 ms, post-dispatch | 346 | 7.2% | 30.9% | 1.93 | 35 plies |
| 2,500 ms, 10 pairs | 320 | 5.9% | 26.3% | 2.13 | 32 plies |

¹ Two runs contain one depth-64 record each (a terminal/forced position); excluding it gives the
value in brackets. Game lengths are counted after the 10-ply opening position the games start from.

Scores: Pikafish won 19–1, 19–1 and 20–0 in the three 500 ms runs and 20–0 at 2,500 ms. Pikafish's reported mean depth
is 45–59 in the same budgets. Nominal depths of the two engines are not comparable, but the order
of magnitude of the gap in *effective* search is what matters here.

**Depth 0 is not a shallow search; it is a non-search.** In `SearchEngine.choose_move`, when the
stop signal arrives before iteration 1 completes, the engine returns
`SearchResult(root_moves[0], …, depth=0)` — `root_moves` is the unsorted legal-move list, so the
move is whatever the generator produced first. In the 500 ms runs, up to one move in four was
played that way. Even at 2,500 ms, 6% were.

Why does iteration 1 not complete? In 12 of the 25 positions where the post-dispatch engine fell
back to depth 0 (replayed from the records), a *complete* depth-1 search costs **2,509–5,379
nodes (median ≈ 2,750; 0.23–0.54 s, median 0.31 s)** on the review machine; a complete depth-2 search costs
3.7K–15.3K nodes (0.40–1.62 s). A 1-ply search should cost on the order of tens to hundreds of
nodes; the rest is quiescence search with no capture ordering by default, no delta/SEE pruning, and
per-node legality and stalemate probing (§3, P2).

### 2.2 Where the time goes

Independent measurement on a 1-vCPU review machine (Python 3.12.3). Node counts reproduce the
project's own (initial position, depth 4: 17,107 here vs. 17,079 recorded), so the machine class is
representative.

| Position | Depth | Nodes | Time | Nodes/s |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| initial | 3 / 4 | 5,489 / 17,107 | 0.28 s / 1.67 s | 19.8K / 10.3K |
| open middlegame | 3 / 4 | 4,268 / 17,865 | 0.32 s / 1.67 s | 13.5K / 10.7K |
| capture-rich | 3 / 4 | 8,909 / 24,109 | 0.56 s / 1.89 s | 15.9K / 12.8K |

`cProfile`, depth 4, open middlegame, 17,865 nodes: **7.40 M Python function calls (≈ 414 per
node)**. Shares of profiled time (cumulative, so they overlap):

| Component | Share |
|---|---:|
| legality probing (`_is_legal_candidate` → `is_in_check` → attack scan) | ≈ 31% |
| pseudo-legal generation (`generate_moves`, incl. one `Move` object per move) | ≈ 28% |
| evaluation (`evaluate`, full 90-square rescan) | ≈ 18% |
| `Enum.__hash__` alone (1.11 M calls; `PieceType`/`Color` are dict keys) | ≈ 9% |
| repetition scan, `Board.is_repetition()` (not in the profile above) | ≈ 4.3 µs/node at 101 plies of history ≈ 5% of ~80 µs/node, linear in game length |

The cost is spread across the whole representation (Python objects, enums, per-move allocation,
per-move legality probe), which is why the long series of micro-optimizations in V0.9.9–V0.10 each bought roughly 3–30% on its own target and
the project's own notes record that full-search wall time often showed no clear improvement above run-to-run variation.

### 2.3 Why the experiments cannot steer

Half-width of a 95% interval on the score of an even match, at 20% draws, for a sample of *n* games:

| Games | 95% half-width |
|---:|---:|
| 8 | ±215 Elo |
| 16 | ±152 Elo |
| 40 | ±96 Elo |
| 60 | ±79 Elo |
| 96 | ±62 Elo |

Needed for a target half-width, and what that costs:

| Target half-width | Games needed | Python @ 500 ms, 1 core | Native @ 100 ms, 8 cores |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| ±90 Elo | 46 | 0.7 h | 1 min |
| ±40 Elo | 232 | 3.5 h | 5 min |
| ±20 Elo | 928 | 14 h | 21 min |
| ±10 Elo | 3,710 | 57 h | 85 min |

(Game cost assumes ~110 plies per game. The native column is a planning figure for V0.12+.)

The project's recorded samples (4–48 opening pairs, i.e. 8–96 games) all sit in the first table. Every forward-pruning
and ordering experiment of V0.10 landed inside its own interval around 50%: null-move 49–56%
(64–96 games), LMR 50% (16 games), aspiration 50% (20), quiet checks 47.5–50% (60), SEE 43.8%
(4 pairs / 8 games), root ordering and MVV-LVA not promoted. These are consistent with "no effect" and equally
consistent with "±40 Elo". They also run at a depth (≈ 2–4 plies in 500 ms) where selective pruning
has little tree to remove. The only experiment that cleanly resolved was depth 4 vs. depth 3
(**+134 Elo**, 30 pairs, 95% +41…+241): depth — i.e. throughput — is the lever with a large effect.

### 2.4 Other verified facts

- **UCCI loses the game's repetition history.** `UCCIEngine._search_worker` rebuilds the search
  board with `board_from_fen(search_fen)`, and `board_from_fen` resets `history`,
  `position_history` and `repetition_history`. Verified by a scripted session: after a `position
  … moves …` line that makes the current position occur three times, the replayed board holds a
  repetition history of length 9; the board handed to `choose_move` holds length 1.
  The Web UI and CLI search the live `Board` and are not affected.
- **Rules.** `Rule.game_result` scores every repetition as a draw and documents that long
  check / long chase adjudication is "kept separate for a later rules layer". Search treats a
  position as repeated only at its third occurrence.
- **Time control.** `go time …` uses `depth = engine.depth` (5) as an iteration cap, so extra clock
  is never used; `go movetime` has no safety margin; a root position that already counts as a
  repetition returns no move.
- **Configuration drift.** Web UI = `SeeSearchEngine(depth 5, LMR, SEE)`; CLI/UCCI =
  `SearchEngine(depth 5, LMR)`; match harness = `SearchEngine(depth=cap)` with LMR off. A SEE flag
  was a silent no-op until the V0.10 follow-up, because the board was never passed to the ordering
  hook.
- **Code mass.** `core/` 1,395 LOC, `engine/` 2,275 (449 of them MCTS), `neural/` 955, versus
  `tools/` 5,385 and `tests/` 7,793. `SearchEngine.__init__` has 33 parameters, 18 boolean feature
  flags, 9 of them default-off. 31 of 65 test files are named after versions, not modules.
- **Human report (V0.9.9).** A 天天象棋 专业1-1 player beat the then depth-2 engine while giving a
  horse and three tempi. Not re-tested since depth 5 became the default.

## 3. Diagnosis: pain points

Ranked by expected effect on playing strength, with the phase that addresses each.

| ID | Pain point | Severity | Addressed in |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Python substrate caps throughput at ~10–30K nodes/s | Critical | V0.12 |
| P2 | Shallow search unreliable in tactical positions; arbitrary-move fallback | Critical | V0.11.2, V0.13 |
| P3 | Real-play correctness gaps (UCCI history, rules, time, config drift) | High | V0.11.2–11.3 |
| P4 | Measurement cannot resolve the effects being chased | High | V0.11.1, V0.12 |
| P5 | Evaluation shallow, non-incremental; plug-in design blocks learning | Medium → High | V0.13, V0.14 |
| P6 | Learning path not yet viable as a strength path | Medium | V0.14, research track |
| P7 | Code, test, documentation and data structure slows iteration | Medium | V0.11.2, V0.11.4 |
| P8 | Missing engine knowledge and features | Low (for now) | V0.13+ |

### P1 — The Python substrate caps throughput (Critical)

*What.* `Board` is a list of lists of heap `Piece` objects; piece type and color are `Enum`s; every
generated move is a `Move` object holding two tuples and two piece references; legality is
"generate pseudo-legal, make, scan rays from the king, unmake" for every move of every node;
`evaluate()` rescans 90 squares; ≈ 414 Python calls per node.

*Evidence.* §2.1–2.2. Depth is worth ≈ +134 Elo per ply at this depth (§2.3), and the engine
completes 2–3 plies where a competitive engine completes tens.

*Why micro-optimization cannot close it.* V0.9.9 doubled the speed and then said so itself: the
remaining time "is spread across the attack detector … the next meaningful gain is architectural".
A pure-Python redesign (flat integer board, no enums, lazy legality after ordering, incremental
evaluation) is estimated at **2–3×** — about one extra ply — against the **≥ 50–100×** a native
core is expected to deliver. These are estimates; the V0.12.0 spike turns them into measurements.

### P2 — Shallow search is unreliable in tactical positions (Critical)

*What.*
(a) Stop before iteration 1 completes → first generated move, depth 0 (§2.1).
(b) An interrupted iteration is discarded entirely, even when the previous best move has already
been re-searched at the new depth.
(c) Quiescence: capture ordering is off by default (`use_mvv_lva=False`; with it off, captures are not
even moved ahead of quiets in the main search); no delta or SEE pruning; each q-node does a check
test, capture generation with legality probes, a `has_legal_move` stalemate probe and a full
evaluation; entries are keyed `(hash, qply)`, which splits otherwise identical positions.
(d) No check extension in the main search.

*Evidence.* §2.1: 26.5% depth-0 moves, 2.5–5.4K nodes for a complete depth-1 search.

*Effect.* A large share of moves in tactical games is arbitrary or ≤ 2 plies; games end in 30–35
plies. This, more than eval tuning, is why a strong opponent punishes the engine immediately.

### P3 — Real-play correctness gaps (High)

(a) **UCCI history loss** (§2.4): in any GUI game the engine can neither avoid nor exploit a
repetition. (b) **Rules**: no long check (长将) / long chase (长捉); a draw by repetition is scored
identically whoever is giving the check; in-tree repetition is only recognized at the third
occurrence; `repetition_count` is an O(game length) scan at every node. (c) **Time**: iteration
cap of 5 under `go time`, no soft/hard limits, no margin. (d) **Configuration drift**: what is
measured is not what is played; a silent no-op flag survived for several versions.

### P4 — The measurement system cannot resolve the effects it chases (High)

Per §2.3: 4–48 opening pairs (8–96 games), ±60–200 Elo; fixed batches with a bootstrap, no sequential test; six
near-duplicate runners (five `compare_*_paired.py`, 1,484 lines, plus `compare_engines.py`, 389)
that each re-implement validation, bootstrap and the Elo formula; "neutral" features accumulate as
opt-in flags; mean completed depth is used as a stand-in for gain (null-move: +0.3–0.4 depth,
0 Elo); the Pikafish runs use parallel workers without CPU pinning or recorded load, so the drop
from 27% to 7% depth-0 across runs cannot be cleanly attributed to code versus machine state; and
the only absolute anchor is an all-or-nothing result (0–20), which gives no gradient to climb.

### P5 — Evaluation is shallow and non-incremental (Medium, High once P1 is fixed)

Material + four piece-square tables (none for king/advisor/elephant) + guard bonuses + open-file
penalty; mobility, pawn structure, coordination and endgame terms exist but are off (no evidence);
no phase interpolation; full rescan per call. The best evaluation result in project history is the
V0.6.4/6.5 calibration (+49/+81 Elo): a linear fit of a handful of weights, near its ceiling. The
`eval_fn(board, color)` plug-in interface is stateless, so it cannot host an incrementally updated
network — part of why the neural evaluator was both slow and weak.

### P6 — The learning path is not yet a viable strength path (Medium)

The neural evaluator (−269 Elo): ~95K positions, MSE on raw centipawns whose standard deviation was
3,061 cp (mate scores included), non-quiet positions, dense numpy inference per node. The policy
MLP and MCTS-with-Python-eval showed no gain over the alpha-beta mainline. The 141,514-game archive
(506,056 distinct positions in the first 20 plies alone; on the order of 10⁷ over whole games, an estimate) is used
only for opening statistics. None of this shows that neural evaluation cannot work here: what was
tried differs from NNUE-class practice in **data scale** (10⁵ vs. typically 10⁷–10⁹+ positions),
**label design** (raw cp vs. win-probability/WDL targets on quiet positions) and **inference
design** (dense vs. incrementally updated accumulators).

### P7 — Structure that slows iteration (Medium)

A 33-parameter constructor with 18 boolean flags; `SeeSearchEngine` re-implements ordering by
subclassing; version-named tests; `tools/` larger than `core/` + `engine/`; 61 doc files (12,551
lines; `roadmap.md` alone is 2,266, about half of it an inline hand-off chain) with the
architecture/design documents frozen at v0.1; stale statements (§10); generated artifacts in git
(`data/` 42 MB, `trainingdata/` 76 MB of archives, `.git` 113 MB).

### P8 — Missing knowledge and features (Low until P1–P4 are fixed)

No endgame knowledge or tablebase; an opening book built from the engine's own weak self-play (a
Pikafish-teacher book scored 53.6% ± 10 over 56 games: the engine cannot follow through); no
pondering; single-threaded.

### What is *not* the problem — keep it

- **The rule core.** 402 tests pass; perft(1–2) from the initial position is asserted. (Depths 3–4
  are listed as constants in `tests/test_perft.py` but not run, and only the initial position is
  covered — thin for a core that will be ported; see V0.12.1.)
- **The search skeleton.** Negamax/PVS/TT/iterative deepening behave as theory predicts
  (effective branching factor ≈ 4.3, V0.9.9). The ideas are right; the engine cannot afford them.
- **The evidence culture.** Paired openings, replay validation, manifests, documented negative
  results: this is the project's strongest asset. The plan amplifies it (SPRT, ladder, evidence
  packages) rather than replacing it.

## 4. Stop list

Effective immediately, until the phase noted:

1. **No more Python hot-path micro-optimizations** except bug fixes (each ≤ 30%; ceiling ≈ 2–3×).
2. **No more < 100-game A/B tests** of forward-pruning/ordering features on the Python engine.
   They cannot resolve the effect (§2.3) and the depth does not let selectivity pay off.
3. **No new `SearchEngine` constructor flags.** New options go through `EngineConfig` and the flag
   registry with an evidence ID (V0.11.2).
4. **No new `compare_*` scripts.** Extend `tools/match.py` (V0.11.1).
5. **No further numpy-MLP evaluation / policy-net iterations** and **no Pikafish-teacher opening
   books** until V0.14 and V0.13 respectively.
6. **No strength claims from depth or node statistics.** Claims come from SPRT gates, the ladder
   and the tactics suite (§6) only.

## 5. Target architecture

### 5.1 Layers

```
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Interfaces     Web UI │ CLI │ UCCI (native exe + Python adapter) │ tools/ │
 └───────────────────────────────▲──────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                 │  EngineConfig · SearchLimits · SearchResult
 ┌───────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Engine API (Python, stable contract)      core selector: native|python|auto│
 └───────▲───────────────────────────────────────────────────▲──────────────┘
         │ pybind11 / C ABI                                    │
 ┌───────┴───────────────────────────┐        ┌───────────────┴─────────────┐
 │ azcore  (native, C++17)           │  diff  │ alphazetacchess  (Python)   │
 │  Position · movegen · RuleSet     │◄──────►│  reference implementation:  │
 │  Evaluator  (HCE → NNUE-lite)     │  tests │  executable spec, oracle    │
 │  Search · TT · time manager       │        │  and no-compiler fallback   │
 └───────────────────────────────────┘        └─────────────────────────────┘
 ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ Measurement & learning (Python)                                           │
 │ match runner + SPRT · Pikafish ladder · AZ-TAC-500 · data gen · trainer    │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 5.2 Principles

1. **Contract first.** `Position`, `Move`, `RuleSet`, `Evaluator`, `EngineConfig`, `SearchLimits`,
   `SearchResult` are specified independently of any implementation (§5.3).
2. **The Python engine becomes the executable specification.** It keeps defining legality, rules and
   the baseline evaluation; the native core must reproduce it bit-for-bit on golden vectors. After
   parity, Python is frozen (bug fixes only) and serves as oracle and no-compiler fallback — it is
   not a second development line.
3. **One configuration source.** One immutable `EngineConfig` and named presets (`play`, `analysis`,
   `test`, `baseline-v0.11`); its hash is written into every game record.
4. **Measurement is infrastructure.** One runner, one SPRT, one ladder, one tactics suite (§6).
5. **Every version stays runnable** (existing project principle): `--core auto` falls back to Python
   with a visible warning when no native build is available.
6. **Evidence-gated defaults.** Nothing is default-on without a registered, accepted gate result.

### 5.3 Core v2 contract (what the native core must expose)

| Object | Contract | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Position | FEN in/out; `make`/`unmake`; legal-move list; `is_check`; 64-bit Zobrist; repetition stack **including the game's real history** | no allocation in make/unmake; history loaded from `position … moves …` |
| Move | compact integer (from-square 7 bits, to-square 7 bits) plus an unambiguous ICCS string form | undo information lives in the stack frame, not in the move |
| RuleSet | `legal(pos)`, `adjudicate(history)`, `repetition_score(path, checks)` | versioned (`simple_draw`, `chinese_v1`); see V0.11.3 |
| Evaluator | `refresh(pos)`, `push(move)`, `pop()`, `evaluate(side)`; integer score | incremental by design: HCE accumulators now, NNUE accumulators later |
| Search | `search(pos, limits, config, on_info) → SearchResult(best, score, depth, nodes, pv)` | releases the GIL; stop flag polled every N nodes |
| EngineConfig | immutable; serializable to UCCI `setoption name … value …` | the same blob drives native and Python |
| UCCI | standalone executable with full-history `position`; `uci`-style id with version and git hash | used by the match runner without Python overhead |

Representation choices that are *not* fixed here and are settled by the V0.12.0 spike: board
layout (padded mailbox with tables vs. 128-bit bitboards — open-source Xiangqi engines use both),
slider attack method, TT entry layout. The contract above is independent of them.

### 5.4 Differential-testing protocol

The Python reference produces versioned golden vectors (V0.12.1); the native core is accepted only
at **zero mismatches**. Two parity levels:

- **P-exact** — TT off, selectivity off, aspiration off, quiescence per specification: the root
  minimax value at depths 1–4 must be bit-identical (alpha-beta value is move-order independent in
  this configuration).
- **P-behavioral** — the `baseline-v0.11` default preset: same best move in ≥ 90% of positions at
  depth 4; every difference explained (ties, TT/graph-history effects) in the version document.

### 5.5 Build, packaging, fallback

CMake project; Python extension built on `pip install` when a toolchain is present; CI builds
Linux and Windows (macOS optional) and runs vectors and the soak test. Without a toolchain,
installation succeeds and `--core auto` selects the Python engine with a warning. The native core
is delivered as *two* artifacts — the importable module and the standalone UCCI executable.

## 6. Strength measurement protocol (applies to every phase)

### 6.1 Metric hierarchy

| Code | Metric | Purpose | Gating? |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | **SPRT between versions** (paired openings) | decide whether a change is accepted | yes |
| M2 | **Pikafish node ladder** | absolute, CPU-speed-independent anchor | yes (rungs gained) |
| M3 | **AZ-TAC-500** tactics / blunder suite | cheap, deterministic proxy for "silly moves"; CI regression | yes |
| M4 | **AZ-FALLBACK-25** | the 25 positions where V0.10 fell back to depth 0 (post-dispatch run) | V0.11–V0.12 |
| M5 | Human sanity games | the original complaint, measured directly | report-only through V0.12; blocking at V0.13 |

**Ladder.** Pikafish as an *opponent only* (not redistributed), limited by `go nodes N`, rungs
N ∈ {1, 3, 10, 30, 100, 300, 1k, 3k, 10k, 30k}. V0.11.1 calibrates the rungs against each other
and reports where V0.10 sits (the first rung it scores < 50% against). Targets are stated as
*rungs gained relative to the V0.11 baseline* so they survive recalibration. If Pikafish exposes
strength-limiting options, they may supplement but not replace node limits.

**AZ-TAC-500.** 500 positions sampled from the game archive at plies 10–120 (dev/test split
300/200, fixed in advance), labelled by Pikafish at depth ≥ 25 and legality-validated: ~100
mate-in-1..3, ~150 win-material, ~150 avoid-blunder (≥ 3 of the top-5 moves lose ≥ 300 cp),
~50 only-move defences, ~50 endgames. Metrics at a fixed time: **solve rate**, **blunder rate**
(chosen move loses ≥ 300 cp versus the best), mean centipawn loss. Whole run ≈ 1 minute at
100 ms/position, so it can run in CI.

### 6.2 Gates (sequential probability ratio test on opening *pairs*)

α = β = 0.05; paired openings with colors swapped; pentanomial likelihood on pair scores.
Defaults, to be validated by V0.11.1's own controls:

| Gate | Use | H0 (Elo ≤) | H1 (Elo ≥) | Max games |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| G1 non-regression | refactors, speed-ups, ports | −10 | 0 | 8,000 |
| G2 feature gain | ordinary search/eval features | 0 | +10 | 8,000 |
| G3 milestone leap | structural changes; time-odds form | +50 | +150 | 1,000 |

Rules: every test is **registered before it starts** (ID, code revision, config hashes, opening
set, seed, gate); reaching the game cap without a decision means *not accepted* and the feature
stays off; a re-run requires a code change. No re-running until it passes.

Time-odds form (for pairs far apart in strength, where equal-time scores saturate): *A at t/k vs B
at t*; acceptance means A scores ≥ 50% — an effective speed-up of at least *k*.

### 6.3 Test conditions

Openings: ≥ 2,000 balanced 8-ply positions from the archive (deduplicated; |Pikafish eval| ≤ 100 cp),
stored with a manifest. Time controls: **TC-slow** 500 ms/move (Python engine), **TC-fast**
100 ms/move, **TC-1s** for confirmation (native). Hardware: one game per physical core, workers ≤
physical cores − 1, affinity set where the OS allows, fixed hash size, a reference-`nps` benchmark
at start, load recorded per game.

### 6.4 Evidence package (per claim)

Reproduction command; game records (JSONL) plus manifest (openings, seed, engine/config hash,
binary hash, machine, affinity, `nps` calibration); replay-validation pass; SPRT result (LLR,
bounds, W/D/L and pair counts); a paragraph in the version document with the honest reading —
including when it is negative. This is the project's existing practice, made uniform.

   occurrences; board seen by `choose_move` 1 / 0. Cause: `_search_worker` →
   `board_from_fen(search_fen)`; `board_from_fen` resets all three histories.
2. **Throughput and profile.** `SearchEngine(depth=3|4)` on three positions; `cProfile` sorted by total
   time on the open middlegame at depth 4 (§2.2). Repetition scan: 200,000 calls of
   `board.is_repetition()` after replaying 100 plies of a recorded game.
3. **Fallback positions.** Replay every game in
   `data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings_post_qsearch_dispatch.jsonl`; at each AlphaZeta move
   whose `search_stats` depth is 0, snapshot the position (25 found; the first 12 measured); run
   `SearchEngine(depth=1)` and `(depth=2)` to completion and record nodes and time (§2.1).
4. **Depth distributions.** Aggregate `search_stats.alphazeta` over the four Pikafish files (§2.1).
5. **Statistical power.** Normal approximation, score variance `0.25 × (1 − draw rate)` at 20% draws,
   slope 694.8 Elo per unit score at 50% (§2.3).
