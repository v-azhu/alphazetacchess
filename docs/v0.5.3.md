# AlphaZetaChess V0.5.3 — Endgame Heuristics from Self-Play Data

## Status

**Implemented, tested, smoke-tested end to end.** Not yet enabled by
default, and its constants are not yet validated against a real,
large self-play corpus — same caveat V0.5.2's opening book carried at
this same point in its own development.

## Design

### The hypothesis: `engine/endgame.py`

Xiangqi endgame theory has a well-known principle: **车赛全局，炮怕
残棋** — the Rook's power holds up (arguably grows, thanks to open
lines) throughout the game, while the Cannon's power *declines* as the
board empties, because a Cannon needs an intervening piece to capture
over, and the endgame is exactly when those screening pieces have
mostly been traded away. This is a genuine, well-established piece of
practical Xiangqi knowledge, not something invented for this
checkpoint — but like every other evaluation constant added in this
project (Connected Pawns' bonus in V0.4.4, Doubled Rooks'/Battery's
bonus in V0.4.5), the exact *magnitude* is a first guess that only
real data can confirm or correct.

`is_endgame(board)`: a single, symmetric material-threshold phase
classifier. Sums Rook(900)+Cannon(450)+Horse(400) "major piece"
material for both sides combined (a phase-classification-only value
table, deliberately independent of `evaluation.MATERIAL_VALUES` so a
future retune of the main material table can't silently shift where
the endgame boundary falls), and calls it endgame once that combined
total drops to ≤2600 — roughly "majors are mostly traded off; a bit
more than one side's worth remains in total." Advisors/Elephants are
excluded (purely defensive, don't change how piece-dependent the
position is); Pawns are excluded too (a position can be pawn-heavy at
any phase, since pawns are rarely traded off early, so counting them
would misclassify a normal middlegame as an endgame).

`endgame_score(board, color)` / `endgame_balance(board,
perspective_color)`: **only** inside the endgame phase, apply a flat
+40 per Rook and −40 per Cannon a side owns, on top of the ordinary
material score `evaluation.py` already computes. Outside the endgame
phase, both return exactly 0 — Rook and Cannon keep their normal
`MATERIAL_VALUES` everywhere else, unmodified.

**Deliberately no king-activity term.** V0.4.2's King Safety and this
module might look like natural neighbors ("king safety early, king
activity late" is a staple Western-chess pattern), but they don't
actually generalize to Xiangqi: unlike a chess King, the Xiangqi
King/General can **never** leave its palace, at any phase of the
game — there is no equivalent of a chess King marching to the center
in the endgame. Deliberately not attempting a translation of a
heuristic that doesn't apply.

Wired into `evaluate()` as `use_endgame_heuristics=False` (disabled by
default, exactly the same pattern as every other V0.4.x/V0.5.x
optional term) and threaded through `SearchEngine`'s constructor and
all four of its internal `evaluate()` call sites (root static eval,
Negamax leaf eval, and both Quiescence eval paths), the same places
`use_piece_coordination` already reaches.

### The validation tool: `selfplay/endgame_analysis.py` + `tools/analyze_endgame.py`

Mirrors V0.5.2's `opening_book.py` in spirit: build the *mechanism*
now and let it run against whatever corpus exists, rather than waiting
for a large real corpus before writing any code — but keep "trusting
the specific constants" as a clearly separate, re-runnable step.

`find_endgame_onset(record)` replays one self-play record's move list
through a fresh `Board`, stopping at the first ply where `is_endgame`
holds, and reports each side's Rook/Cannon/Horse counts at that
moment. `summarize_endgame_outcomes(records)` aggregates across many
records: how many games ever reach the endgame phase, the average ply
they reach it at, and — the actual hypothesis test — among games with
a non-tied Rook/Cannon edge at endgame onset (weighted by this
module's own `ENDGAME_ROOK_BONUS`/`ENDGAME_CANNON_PENALTY`, so it's
measuring exactly the thing the constants are a bet on, not a
different proxy), how often the favored side actually won.

`tools/analyze_endgame.py`: CLI (`--input`) that loads records via
V0.5.1's `load_records` and prints the summary, with an explicit
"sample too small to draw real conclusions" note whenever fewer than
20 decided endgame-onset positions are available — which, at this
checkpoint, is every corpus that exists so far.

## Correctness gates (`tests/test_endgame_v053.py`, 13 tests)

1. The starting position is not classified as endgame.
2. A position with only one Rook per side (well under the threshold)
   is classified as endgame.
3. Bare kings (zero major material) is classified as endgame.
4. `endgame_score` is exactly 0 outside the endgame phase, regardless
   of piece counts.
5. `endgame_score` correctly sums the Rook bonus and Cannon penalty
   when in the endgame phase.
6. `endgame_balance` is exactly antisymmetric between colors.
7. Disabling the flag (explicitly or by default) reproduces V0.5.2's
   evaluation exactly — the same "baseline preservation" gate every
   other optional term has.
8. Enabling the flag changes the evaluation by exactly the expected
   delta on a constructed position.
9. `SearchEngine(use_endgame_heuristics=...)` reaches `evaluate()`
   correctly through `_quiescence` (the same check
   `test_piece_coordination_v045.py` uses for its own toggle).
10. `find_endgame_onset` returns `None` for a record whose moves never
    reach the endgame phase.
11. `find_endgame_onset` finds the correct ply on a hand-built,
    capture-heavy synthetic move sequence (verified independently by
    replaying it against a real `Board()` before writing the test —
    `Board.move()` doesn't validate legality, so a fabricated capture
    sequence is a legitimate, fast way to exercise this without
    needing real search-generated games).
12. `summarize_endgame_outcomes` correctly counts reached vs.
    never-reached games and computes the right average onset ply.
13. `summarize_endgame_outcomes` credits a decided endgame-onset
    position to the correct side, both when that side goes on to win
    and when it goes on to lose.

Combined with every other targeted V0.4.x/V0.5.x test file: **122/122
green.**

## Smoke test (this session)

```
python tools/self_play.py --games 6 --depth 1 --max-moves 40  → 0/6 games reached endgame
python tools/self_play.py --games 3 --depth 1 --max-moves 100 → 1/3 games reached endgame
  Average endgame onset ply: 70.0
  Positions with a Rook/Cannon edge at onset: 1 (tied: 0)
    Favored side won 0/1 (0%), lost 0, drew 1
    NOTE: sample too small to draw real conclusions -- collect more self-play data before trusting this number.
```

Confirms the full pipeline works end to end (self-play → endgame
onset detection → edge-vs-outcome summary), and incidentally confirms
something useful about the threshold itself: at `depth=1` with a
40-move cap, games essentially never reach it — the endgame phase, as
`is_endgame` defines it, genuinely does correspond to a late-game
state, not an artifact of a threshold set too high. Not discarded as
"just a smoke test" — this is real evidence the phase boundary is in
approximately the right place, even though the win-rate correlation
itself still needs a much larger corpus to mean anything.

## Known limitation

Exactly the one flagged as a risk going in: **the +40/−40 magnitudes
are theory-grounded, not data-validated.** The tooling to validate
them (`tools/analyze_endgame.py`) is built, tested, and confirmed to
work end to end — but with only 1 decided endgame-onset position
across this session's smoke-test corpus, there is nowhere near enough
data yet to say whether +40/−40 is the right magnitude, the right
sign, or matters at all in practice. `use_endgame_heuristics` stays
disabled by default until that changes.

A second, smaller limitation: `is_endgame`'s combined (not per-color)
threshold means a position where one side has traded off everything
while the other still has a full complement of majors is *not*
classified as endgame if the combined total is still above 2600 —
this is a deliberate first-version simplification (see
`engine/endgame.py`'s docstring), not a bug, but worth revisiting if
analysis ever shows lopsided-material positions are where the Rook/
Cannon effect actually matters most.
