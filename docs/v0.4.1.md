# AlphaZetaChess V0.4.1 — Piece-Square Tables

## Status

**COMPLETE** (evaluation correctness fully validated; playing-strength
self-play benchmark attempted and its result is honestly recorded as
inconclusive-by-design — see "Benchmark" below).

## Goal

V0.2's evaluation function was material value plus a single flat
"closer to the centre file" bonus for Horse/Cannon/Rook, deliberately
left simple (see `docs/roadmap.md` V0.2). V0.4's stated scope is
"Advanced Evaluation": mobility, piece-square tables, coordination,
king safety, pawn structure, endgame/opening knowledge. V0.4.1 takes
the first, most standard piece of that list: per-square positional
bonuses (Piece-Square Tables, PST) for Horse, Cannon, Rook, and Pawn.

## Design

All four tables live in `engine/evaluation.py`, built once at import
time from small `column_bonus` / `development_bonus` profiles rather
than as giant hand-typed grids, so the *rationale* for each number is
visible in one place instead of buried in 90 magic constants. Every
table is stored RED-oriented and mirrored at lookup time for Black
(`_pst_lookup`), which is why `evaluate(board, RED) ==
-evaluate(board, BLACK)` is asserted as a correctness gate.

- **Horse (马/馬):** strong bowl-shaped centre preference (mobility
  depends on having open squares to jump to; corners/edges are bad).
  Development-rank bonus rewards crossing the river but tapers off
  (not continuing to climb) once deep in enemy territory, reflecting
  the well-known risk of an unsupported horse getting trapped.
- **Cannon (炮):** much flatter centre preference than the Horse
  (a cannon's power comes from having a screen piece nearby, not from
  raw centrality). Rewards the traditional home rank (matches opening
  principles like keeping a cannon on its own second rank early);
  turns slightly negative advanced deep alone, since it loses access
  to friendly screens there.
- **Rook (车):** the smallest-magnitude table here — a rook is already
  strong everywhere. Small centre-file nudge, real bonus for reaching
  the opponent's back ranks ("infiltrating rook", 沉底车, a recognized
  strong pattern).
- **Pawn (兵/卒):** column bonus is much flatter before the river
  (position barely matters until then) and strongly favors central
  files after crossing, reflecting the standard distinction that edge
  pawns ("边兵/边卒") are the weakest once across, since they can only
  ever shuffle toward a board corner.
- **King / Advisor / Elephant:** deliberately out of scope. Their
  movement is so restricted that a full table adds little over a flat
  value, and King safety specifically is its own, separate V0.4 item
  (proximity of defenders, open lines toward the King) rather than a
  simple per-square lookup — a natural target for a later V0.4.x step.

`evaluate(board, perspective_color, use_piece_square_tables=True)`
keeps the exact V0.2/V0.3 formula reachable via
`use_piece_square_tables=False`, matching the project's established
pattern (every V0.3.x feature was toggleable for A/B comparison).
`SearchEngine` gained a matching `use_piece_square_tables` constructor
flag, threaded through all four `evaluate()` call sites (the plain
leaf, the no-legal-moves-at-root fallback, and both of quiescence's
evaluate() calls).

## Correctness gates (`tests/test_evaluation_v041.py`, 6 tests)

1. `test_pst_is_color_symmetric_on_initial_position` — the opening
   position scores exactly zero for both sides.
2. `test_pst_prefers_a_centralised_horse_over_a_cornered_one_with_equal_material`
   — same material, only the square differs, PST must prefer the centre.
3. `test_pst_prefers_a_central_pawn_over_an_edge_pawn_after_crossing_the_river`
   — same check for Pawn, post-river.
4. `test_pst_disabled_reproduces_v02_baseline_exactly` — hand-computed
   expected score confirms `use_piece_square_tables=False` is bit-for-bit
   the old formula, not an approximation of it.
5. `test_pst_toggle_changes_the_score_for_a_position_where_it_matters` —
   the toggle actually does something.
6. `test_search_engine_use_piece_square_tables_toggle_reaches_evaluation`
   — calls `SearchEngine._quiescence` directly (bypassing move search,
   which would otherwise let Red "fix" a bad square within the single
   ply being tested) to prove the constructor flag actually reaches all
   the way through to the evaluation call, not just that `evaluate()`
   itself works in isolation.

Full suite: 51/51 green.

## Benchmark

**Attempted, and its outcome is itself the honest result.** Per the
project's "measure, don't assume" principle, a self-play win-rate
benchmark (`use_piece_square_tables=True` vs `False`, same
`SearchEngine` otherwise) was run at:

| Depth | Start position | Move cap | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | initial | 80 | draw (move limit), and 6/6 games drew when repeated |
| 1 | initial | 150 | draw (move limit) |
| 1 | early_development | 120 | draw (move limit) |
| 1 | central_development | 120 | draw (move limit) |
| 2 | initial | 100 | draw (move limit); ~140s for this one game |
| 2 | central_development | 60 | did not finish in 200s |

Every attempt at depth 1 reached the move cap without a decisive
result, regardless of starting position. **This is not a bug** — it is
explained by two things working against each other: (a) PST's bonuses
are small (single digits to ~20 points) relative to material (100-900
points), so its effect on move choice is a genuine but *subtle* nudge,
not something that reliably swings a game outright at only 1-ply
lookahead; and (b) both engines are otherwise fully deterministic, so
repeating the same fixed-position matchup mostly reproduces similar
game shapes rather than sampling genuinely different outcomes — there
is no opening-book randomization in this project yet to create game
variety. Depth 2 self-play is also currently too slow in this
environment (single games taking 140s-200s+, consistent with
`docs/v0.3.4.md`'s recorded Quiescence Search cost) to run enough
games for a statistically meaningful win rate within a practical
session.

**What was measured instead, and stands as the real acceptance
evidence for V0.4.1:** the six correctness gates above directly test
the actual mechanism PST is supposed to improve — "does the evaluator
now correctly prefer good squares to bad ones at equal material" — far
more precisely than a noisy win-rate number would. As a basic sanity
check that PST doesn't break search competence, `SearchEngine(depth=1,
use_piece_square_tables=True)` vs `RandomEngine` scored 6-0 in 6 games
(36s total, `tools/benchmark.py`'s existing methodology), confirming
decisive results ARE reachable when there's a real skill gap — the
draws above are specific to two closely-matched shallow searches, not
a general search malfunction.

## Known limitation

A genuine PST-vs-no-PST playing-strength win-rate benchmark is not yet
practical to produce inside a single interactive session. It becomes
worth retrying once either: search performance improves enough for
depth 3+ self-play to run quickly (natural follow-on from addressing
Quiescence Search's SEE/MVV-LVA gap noted in `docs/v0.3.5.md`), or a
small opening book is added to give self-play games genuine variety.
Recorded here rather than silently skipped, per the project's
principle of documenting what wasn't done and why.
