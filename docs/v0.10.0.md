# V0.10.0 — Search Correctness and Horizon Cost

## Scope

This checkpoint follows the strength-oriented review of the current project. It fixes
search correctness edge cases first, strengthens objective tactical regression checks,
then takes a measured, behavior-preserving performance improvement. It does not claim
that these changes alone make the engine stronger overall.

## Search correctness

### Repetition

`Board.position_history` includes search-only null moves, so it cannot safely serve as
the actual-game repetition record. `Board.repetition_history` now tracks only the
initial position and positions reached through real moves; null moves do not add
repetition occurrences. `board_from_fen()` initializes both histories consistently.

Alpha-beta and quiescence check for repetition before accepting a TT score. A repeated
position with legal moves scores as a draw (0), and the repetition result is not stored
as a position-only TT value. As in `Rule.game_result()`, checkmate/stalemate takes
precedence over repetition. A root with no legal moves is scored as a loss. MCTS treats
repetition leaves as draws and does not search from an already-repeated root.

### Quiescence check evasions and TT separation

The quiescence ply cap no longer causes a position where the side to move is in check
to be statically evaluated. Check evasions continue to be searched until the side is no
longer in check; repeated positions terminate as draws. This avoids stand-pat evaluation
of an illegal-to-ignore check while retaining the cap on ordinary capture sequences.

Quiescence TT keys include the quiescence ply. The amount of remaining quiescence search
depends on that ply, so scores searched with different remaining budgets must not reuse
one another's entries. The separate key also prevents quiescence entries from colliding
with ordinary alpha-beta entries for the same board hash.

## Tactical regression coverage

The existing objective tactical fixtures now check the engine's behavior, not just the
fixture geometry:

- select a legal immediate mate when one exists;
- select an objectively safe capture in the free-capture position;
- avoid the capture that permits the opponent's immediate recapture.

The tests independently enumerate the property being checked instead of asserting an
unverified engine-preferred move. Existing diagnostics additionally cover board-state
restoration and search through depth 4.

## Horizon performance

At depth zero, when quiescence is enabled, `_negamax()` previously generated all legal
moves and then immediately called quiescence, which generated legal moves again. It now
delegates directly to quiescence; quiescence remains responsible for terminal-position
detection. When quiescence is disabled, the prior depth-zero terminal check is preserved.

Single-run iterative-deepening measurements on the checked reference positions:

| Position | Depth | Before | After | Nodes before/after | Score/move |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| initial | 3 | 4.798s | 2.735s | 5,115 / 5,115 | same |
| early_development | 3 | 6.063s | 3.666s | 5,512 / 5,512 | same |
| central_development | 3 | 3.193s | 1.953s | 4,485 / 4,485 | same |

These are local, single-run timings and are sensitive to machine load. The consistent
roughly 39–43% reduction at depth 3 is evidence for this optimization on the tested
positions, not a general throughput guarantee. The depth 2 results were noisier.

## Depth-4 vs. depth-3 strength checks

Ran 20 alternating-color games with opening randomization disabled. Depth 4 played Red
in 10 games and Black in 10. The result was 10 wins, 10 losses, no draws: 50% score and
estimated Elo difference 0 for depth 4. The game records are in
`data/v0.10_depth4_vs_depth3.jsonl`.

A follow-up ran 100 alternating-color games with default opening randomization: each
engine could choose a random legal move during the first 10 plies with probability 0.3
per ply. Depth 4 played Red in 50 games and Black in 50. From depth 4's perspective:

| Games | Wins | Losses | Draws | Score rate | Elo estimate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 60 | 37 | 3 | 61.5% | +81 |

By color, depth 4 scored 26 wins, 22 losses, and 2 draws as Red, versus 34 wins,
15 losses, and 1 draw as Black. The opening randomizations were not paired between
colors, so this split may reflect opening-sample variation as well as color effects.

A 20,000-resample game-level bootstrap (draws scored as 0.5) gives a 95% percentile
interval of 52.0–71.0% for score rate, or approximately +14 to +156 Elo after
transforming the endpoints. There were 97 distinct first-10-ply move sequences. The
three recorded draws all reached the 150-ply move cap; they were not separately
adjudicated as board-theoretic draws. The 100 records, in
`data/v0.10_depth4_vs_depth3_multiopening_100.jsonl`, were replayed from the standard
initial board and passed move-count, turn-order, and legal-move validation. The
recorded time from the first game's completion to the last was about 5 hours 18 minutes.

The original 20-game and randomized 100-game results are separate samples. The latter
is suggestive evidence in favor of depth 4 on this randomized-opening setup, not a
definitive or general Elo claim. Its interval reflects game-to-game resampling within
this one sample and does not capture all uncertainty in the opening distribution. A
stronger follow-up would use an independently generated fixed opening set and play
each opening twice with colors mirrored; the paired follow-up below controls color on
the sampled openings.

### Paired-opening color control

game and Black in the other:

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

### Pikafish opponent check with paired colors

A local Pikafish UCI binary and NNUE network were smoke-tested, then used through a new
`PikafishEngine` adapter. Matching nominal depth was rejected for the full match after
a pilot showed radically different search costs (initial-position depth 4 took about
2.4 seconds in AlphaZeta and about 1 ms in Pikafish). Instead, both engines were given
500 ms per move: AlphaZeta used iterative deepening with a 64-ply depth cap and a
timer-driven stop event; Pikafish used UCI `go movetime 500`.

Ten distinct 10-ply positions were sampled from the 3,957-game imported corpus with
seed `20261005`. Each position was played twice with colors reversed (20 games total):

| Games | Pikafish wins | Losses | Draws | Score rate | Elo-style estimate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 19 | 1 | 0 | 95.0% | +512 |

Pikafish scored **10 wins, 0 losses as Red**, and **9 wins, 1 loss as Black**. The
paired bootstrap resampled openings 20,000 times and produced a 95% score interval
of **85–100%**. The upper endpoint corresponds to an unbounded Elo value; the +512
figure is only the point estimate, not a precise rating difference.

All games passed replay validation for legal moves, side-to-move, final result, move
limit, engine configuration, and paired starting positions. They ended decisively in
605 total plies. The records include per-move elapsed time and reported search depth:
AlphaZeta averaged **505.8 ms** and depth **1.36** (including depth-0 fallbacks);
Pikafish averaged **446.7 ms** and depth **47.59**. Equal nominal time is therefore
only approximate (AlphaZeta's Python timer can overshoot), and the large depth gap
shows this is a comparison of overall implementation strength under similar
wall-clock budgets, not an isolated algorithm or evaluation-function comparison.
This remains a small, sample-dependent result from 10 opening pairs.

The JSONL data and manifest are
`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings.jsonl` and
`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings.jsonl.manifest.json`. To reproduce

### Quiescence legal-move filtering

Profiling then identified avoidable work at non-check quiescence nodes: the search
generated all legal moves before retaining captures only. `Rule` now returns legal
captures together with a flag indicating whether any legal move exists, probing quiet
moves only until one legal quiet is found. This retains correct stalemate handling;
in-check nodes still generate every legal evasion. Repetition and move-limit behavior
are unchanged.

In paired local runs of iterative depth 3 on the three reference positions, scores and
node counts were identical before and after. Mean runtime improved from 0.635 to
0.273 seconds on the initial position, 0.885 to 0.390 seconds on early development,
and 0.519 to 0.228 seconds on central development (2.27–2.32x faster). Initial
position iterative depth 4 took 1.16 seconds after the change. Some searches returned
a different best move at the same score, consistent with tied root moves and changed
tie ordering; tactical and score checks remained green.

A fresh 500 ms-per-move match then used 10 new, non-overlapping openings (seed
`20261007`, 20 games, colors swapped). Pikafish again scored **19 wins, 1 loss, 0
draws** (95%; paired bootstrap interval **85–100%**). AlphaZeta's mean reported depth
rose from 1.36 in the earlier sample to **1.77**; its mean observed time was 505.8 ms.
Pikafish scored 9-1 as Red and 10-0 as Black. All games passed move replay and pairing
validation. This modest depth change did not change the aggregate result in this small
sample. The data and manifest are
`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings_post_qsearch.jsonl` and
`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings_post_qsearch.jsonl.manifest.json`.

The next profile found avoidable construction and dispatch costs in that same path.
Added capture-only pseudo-legal generation so quiet `Move` objects are not constructed
when quiescence only needs captures, and replaced the per-piece handler-dictionary
allocation with direct type dispatch. Legal capture sets were cross-checked against
the full legal list for both sides at three opening stages. This follow-up improved
iterative depth-3 runtime a further 15–17% over the prior version on the three
reference positions: 0.254 to 0.215 seconds (initial), 0.367 to 0.310 seconds (early
development), and 0.230 to 0.192 seconds (central development), averaged over three
runs. Iterative depth 4 from the initial position took about 1.06 seconds.

A third disjoint 10-opening 500 ms-per-move paired sample (seed `20261008`) scored
**20 wins, 0 losses, 0 draws for Pikafish**, split 10-0 in each color. All games
passed full replay validation. AlphaZeta averaged reported depth **1.93** at 507.7 ms
per move; Pikafish averaged depth 49.52 at 444.7 ms. Since all 10 opening pairs were
sweeps, the paired bootstrap interval is degenerate at 100–100%; it cannot quantify
the risk of unseen losses, and no finite Elo estimate exists at a 100% score. This
sample does not demonstrate a playing-strength improvement for AlphaZeta. Data and
manifest:
`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings_post_qsearch_dispatch.jsonl` and
`data/v0.10_pikafish_vs_search_500ms_10openings_post_qsearch_dispatch.jsonl.manifest.json`.

## Follow-up: SEE activation and search-time trade-off

The Web UI configures `SeeSearchEngine(use_see=True)`, but the base search previously
did not pass the current board into its move-ordering hook. SEE therefore received no
position and was silently inactive. The board is now passed through, and a regression
test confirms that SEE evaluates multiple capture candidates. This restores the
configured behavior; it is not evidence of a playing-strength gain.

At depth 4 from the initial position, SEE and baseline chose the same move and score,
but SEE took 1.549s and 22,652 nodes versus 1.106s and 17,079 nodes. In a capture-rich
position, SEE took 0.036s and 1,262 nodes versus 0.041s and 1,406 nodes; it was not
called because the position had only one capture candidate. A 4-opening / 8-game,
500 ms paired pilot scored 43.8% for SEE. This sample is far too small to infer a
strength difference.

A depth-5 iterative-deepening benchmark took 4.350s and 137,851 cumulative nodes from
the initial position; depth 4 took about 1.06s in the earlier benchmark. Depth 5 also
took 5.592s in early development and 5.712s in central development. These measurements
show a substantial response-time cost and do not justify changing the default depth.

Null-move pruning remains disabled by default. Its 4-opening / 8-game pilot also
scored 43.8%, an inconclusive result. An unbounded depth-4 SEE comparison was stopped
after more than 10 minutes without completing a game and is not a match result.
Following the SEE wiring and recapture-path changes, the complete suite passes
**361 tests**.

## Follow-up: principal-reply investigation

Added `tools/trace_principal_variation.py` to search root candidates independently
and report TT-derived reply lines, captures, and the static and quiescence-boundary
evaluations. The diagnostic validates traced moves and restores the original board.
Its lines may contain TT bounds and its quiescence score is recomputed at the end of
the line, so neither is guaranteed to be the exact principal variation/leaf from the
root search.

The trace corrected an earlier description: opening moves `(1,2)->(1,9)` and
`(7,2)->(7,9)` capture horses, not rooks. Both are immediately recaptured by a rook;
their score changes from -60 at depth 3 to -160 at depth 4. In the tactical-exchange
fixture, `(4,2)->(4,6)` scores +190 at depth 3, while depth 4 reveals a black-cannon
capture `(4,7)->(4,3)` of the red pawn behind the red cannon and scores 0. These
traced paths explain the specific score swings as horizon effects, but do not
establish a general search defect or support adding a selective extension. The
focused tracer tests pass **4/4**; the full suite passes **361 tests**.

## Follow-up: root-ordering experiments

Two root-move ordering alternatives were benchmarked against the current
iterative-deepening search, using three repeated runs on initial, early-development,
and central-development positions. Static one-ply root ranking increased depth-4
median nodes from 17,079 / 17,350 / 16,887 to 34,334 / 50,488 / 41,314 and increased
median time from 1.051 / 1.333 / 1.121 seconds to 1.599 / 2.618 / 2.016 seconds.
Scores matched; one tied-score case selected a different move.

Ordering all root moves by their scores from the previous iteration preserved the
same move and score in all six depth-3/4 cases, but increased depth-4 median nodes to
19,358 / 20,525 / 17,504 and median time to 1.155 / 1.530 / 1.193 seconds. Depth-3
results were mixed. Both prototypes were rejected; current previous-best root ordering
remains unchanged because neither alternative improved the measured workload.

The existing optional MVV-LVA capture ordering was also rechecked at depths 3–4 on
the same positions (three runs per case). All six cases preserved the baseline score
and best move. Depth-4 node counts changed from 17,079 to 16,006 initially, 17,350 to
24,649 in early development, and 16,887 to 17,087 in central development. Since the
benefit was position-dependent and one case regressed substantially, with no paired
strength evidence, MVV-LVA remains opt-in.

## Follow-up: aspiration windows

Implemented root aspiration windows centered on the last completed iterative search
score. The default half-window is 100 evaluation points; an out-of-window result
triggers a full-window re-search at the same depth, so the next iteration receives an
exact score. Fixed-depth-only searches continue to use a full window. Cancellation
keeps the previous completed iteration. Reproduce comparisons with
`tools/benchmark_search.py --depths 4 5 --repeat 3 --aspiration-window 100` and compare
against `--aspiration-window 10000`.

The three-run depth-4 benchmark reduced time/nodes from 1.172s / 17,079 to 0.898s /
14,668 initially; 1.403s / 17,350 to 1.210s / 16,469 in early development; and
1.205s / 16,887 to 1.171s / 16,884 in central development. Scores matched throughout;
one tied-best move differed in early development.

At depth 5, initial-position time/nodes fell from 4.945s / 137,851 to 3.297s /
97,574, and early-development from 6.106s / 130,259 to 3.673s / 69,142. Central
development was essentially unchanged: 5.945s / 145,053 versus 6.093s / 144,927.
All scores matched, though one tied-best move differed. A 500 ms stop-time check
reached depth 3 in every run for both settings, with the same move and score. The
optimization is promising for runtime in some positions but has not demonstrated
greater depth under this short time budget or a strength gain.

A separate fixed-time decision sample used eight distinct 10-ply openings selected
from the external-game corpus (seed `20261005`), searching each position for 3.5
seconds. Baseline and aspiration selected the same move and completed the same depth
in all eight positions (depths 2–4); mean node count fell from 14,905 to 14,270
(4.3%). This modest throughput result is not evidence of improved move quality or
playing strength and did not produce an extra completed iteration on those midgame
positions. A separate three-run 3.5-second check on the initial and
early-development reference positions completed depth 5 with aspiration and depth 4
with the full-window control in every run. Thus an extra ply is possible in some
positions under that budget, but the result is position-dependent; game-level testing
is still required to establish any playing-strength gain.

Aspiration tests cover fail-high and fail-low re-search, narrow-window parity,
depth-4 full-window score parity, fixed-depth behavior, alpha-beta-disabled search,
invalid-window validation, and cancellation compatibility. The complete suite passes
**373 tests**.
